# Chart Reading — Beginner Fundamentals

Goal: be able to look at any chart and describe *what happened* and *what the market is doing right now* before you ever place a trade.

## 1. The candlestick (your basic unit of information)

Each candle shows four prices for a fixed time period (1 min, 1 hour, 1 day, etc.):

- **Open** — price at the start of the period
- **High** — highest price during the period
- **Low** — lowest price during the period
- **Close** — price at the end of the period

- Green/white candle → Close > Open (buyers won that period)
- Red/black candle → Close < Open (sellers won that period)
- The thin lines above/below the body are **wicks** — they show rejection. A long wick means price tried to go further and got pushed back.

## 2. Timeframes

The same asset looks different depending on the timeframe. Rules of thumb:

| Timeframe | Use for |
|---|---|
| 1m–15m | Very short-term/day trading entries, very noisy |
| 1H–4H | Swing/day trading structure, most beginner-friendly |
| Daily | Overall trend, most reliable signals, fewer false moves |
| Weekly | Big-picture context, long-term trend |

**Beginner rule:** always check the Daily chart first for the overall trend before looking at a smaller timeframe for entries. Trading against the higher-timeframe trend is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

## 3. Trend — the single most important concept

- **Uptrend** = series of Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL)
- **Downtrend** = series of Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL)
- **Range/sideways** = price bouncing between a ceiling and floor, no clear HH/HL or LH/LL

You identify trend by drawing lines connecting the swing highs and swing lows — no indicator required. Indicators (below) just help confirm what your eyes should already see.

## 4. Support and resistance

- **Support** = a price level where buying pressure has repeatedly stopped declines
- **Resistance** = a price level where selling pressure has repeatedly stopped advances
- Old resistance often becomes new support once broken, and vice versa
- Draw these as horizontal lines at price levels that were touched 2+ times

## 5. Core indicators worth learning first (don't use more than 2-3 at once)

- **Moving Average (SMA/EMA)** — smooths price to show trend direction. A 50-period and 200-period MA crossing is a classic (lagging) trend signal.
- **RSI (Relative Strength Index)** — momentum oscillator 0–100. Above 70 = "overbought," below 30 = "oversold." Best used to spot divergence, not as a standalone buy/sell signal.
- **Volume** — confirms conviction. A breakout on high volume is more trustworthy than one on low volume.
- **MACD** — trend + momentum combo, useful for confirming trend changes.

Avoid stacking 6+ indicators — it causes "analysis paralysis" and most indicators are just different views of the same price data.

## 6. A few high-value candlestick patterns

- **Engulfing candle** — a candle whose body completely covers the prior candle's body; signals a potential reversal, especially at support/resistance.
- **Doji** — open ≈ close, long wicks either side; signals indecision, often before a reversal.
- **Pin bar / hammer** — long wick on one side, small body; signals rejection of that price area.

Patterns are weak in isolation — they matter mainly when they occur at a meaningful level (support/resistance, trendline, or moving average).

## 7. Step-by-step: how to read any chart

1. Zoom out. What's the trend on the Daily/Weekly? (up, down, range)
2. Mark the nearest major support and resistance levels.
3. Zoom into your trading timeframe. Where is price relative to those levels right now?
4. Check volume — is it confirming the current move or fading?
5. Only now consider indicators for confirmation, not as the primary signal.
6. Ask: "If I'm wrong, where would that become obvious?" — that's your stop-loss level, decided *before* the trade.

## Practice exercise

Pick 5 charts (any instrument) you've never seen the outcome of. For each, without looking further right, write down: trend, nearest support/resistance, and where you'd place a stop if you entered here. Then scroll right and check if your read was correct. Repeat with 20+ charts before risking real money — this pattern-recognition repetition is what actually builds skill, not reading theory alone.
